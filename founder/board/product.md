# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

Screening only: no CRM data, one working tenant (the founder's own company, Gold Hands), external trials exist, and there are zero paying external customers. Nothing below treats the product as proven.

## What is strong
- The job is concrete and painful on its face: a 1-10 technician home-service shop loses work when nobody answers the phone or follows up a lead. "Replace the human dispatcher" is a clear job, not a vague AI promise.
- The product is already running in a real operation (Gold Hands). That is a real-world test bed, so the experience can be tuned against live calls, leads and jobs instead of a demo. It is not market proof, because the founder is the only user and has no reason to churn.
- Self-serve trial with no card means the first minutes with the product can be designed end to end, with no sales call hiding rough edges.
- A narrow buyer (US owner-operators with 1-10 techs) is a sharp target for a focused product, if the product stays focused.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. No focus: the pitch is "AI platform that runs leads and dispatch", and the feature set per tier is not even fixed. Workiz, Jobber and Housecall Pro are full field-service suites, and AI dispatchers are also in the category. A product that tries to be a suite and an AI dispatcher at once loses to both. Until one sentence says the one thing it does brilliantly, there is no product to judge. The three tiers ($99 / $199 / $399) are a symptom: tiers were set before the core job was.
2. The first-five-minutes experience is unproven for a stranger. Trials exist but nobody has paid, so trial-to-paid, and the reason people stop, is unknown. A trial user is a busy owner with no card on file and no time. If setup needs connecting phone numbers, calendars, a price book and service areas before anything useful happens, they leave before seeing value. Because it is the founder's own company that works, the working tenant says almost nothing about onboarding a stranger.
3. Seams and trust: dispatch touches the customer's own customers. A wrong booking, a missed emergency call, a bad quote or a robotic voice damages the owner's reputation, not just the software's. Every handoff (AI to human, lead to schedule, schedule to technician, job to invoice) is where this breaks, and an owner who gets burned once will not give a second trial. Whether the product has a visible, easy human override is not known.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is the one thing NeurLead does that an owner would pay for on its own? What are you willing to cut to get there?
- What does a trial user see and feel in the first five minutes, and what must be true for them to see value before they close the tab?
- How many external trials exist, how many finished setup, and how many used it for a real lead? (Count only; no number is assumed here. This needs the product's own data.)
- Why did no trial convert to a paid plan so far: price, missing function, trust, or no activation? Have you spoken to the ones who left?
- What exactly differs between $99, $199 and $399, and would one tier with one price be clearer to test first?
- Where does the AI hand off to a human, and what happens when it is wrong? Who sees the mistake first, the owner or the customer?
- Which parts of the Gold Hands workflow are product and which are the founder's personal manual fixes that would not exist for another owner?
- How does the product stand against Workiz, Jobber, Housecall Pro and other AI dispatchers on the one job you pick? Open question, answered by /founder-competitors.
- Would owners actually buy this, at what price, and what would make them say no? Open question, answered by /founder-consumer (simulated) and then by real pre-sales.
- What does one customer cost to acquire and keep versus the monthly fee, and what is the voice/AI cost per lead handled? Open questions, answered by /founder-cfo.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Write the one-sentence product (the job, the buyer, the result) and cut every feature and tier that does not serve it. Fix the feature list per tier, or reduce to a single plan, before any further spend.
- Get at least a small number of external owners to pay real money (even a discounted founding price) and use the product on real leads. Zero paying external customers is the gating fact; until it changes, spend stays near zero. The number and price to aim for come from /founder-cfo and /founder-consumer, not from this memo.
- Instrument the trial: measure who finishes setup, who handles a real lead, and who comes back in week two. Reduce setup to the minimum that delivers a first useful result.
- Define and ship the failure handling: a clear human override, a visible log of what the AI did, and a rule for emergencies and unclear requests, tested before more trials are opened.
- Run /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer, then /founder-cfo, and re-score. If paying customers do not appear after those steps, the vote becomes PASS.
