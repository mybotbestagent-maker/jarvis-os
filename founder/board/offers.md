# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

Scope: quick screening, no CRM data, no external paying customers. I score the business as briefed, not the founder.

## What is strong
- **The pain is real and expensive for the buyer.** An owner with 1-10 technicians who misses a call or answers a lead late loses a job. The dream outcome is easy to name: "every lead answered and every job booked without hiring a dispatcher." This is the strongest part of the business.
- **The buyer has money and the price is small against the pain.** $99 / $199 / $399 a month is small next to one lost job. The three-tier ladder gives room to move the price up if the outcome is proven. Whether the price is right is an open question (see below), but the structure is sound.
- **Low effort to try.** A 14-day trial with no card removes the sacrifice of starting. That helps the effort and time parts of the value equation.
- **One real operating tenant.** The founder runs the product inside an actual home-service company (Gold Hands). That is a live test of whether it works in a real dispatch operation, and it is the only evidence available. It is evidence of function, not of demand.
- **Hot category.** AI dispatch for trades is a category buyers are starting to hear about, so the pitch does not need to explain the problem from zero. (How fast the category is actually growing is an open question for /founder-competitors.)

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Zero paying external customers: no proof the outcome is likely.** This is the likelihood part of the value equation, and it is empty. Nobody outside the founder's own company has paid. External trials exist, but a free trial with no card proves only that people will click. Until a stranger pays and stays, the business has not shown that the promised outcome happens for someone other than its author. The single own-company tenant is a biased test: the founder knows the product, the data and the workflow. Everything else on this page is conditional on fixing this.
2. **The offer is not yet distinguishable from the category.** The competitors named in the brief (Workiz, Jobber, Housecall Pro and other AI dispatchers) already sell to the same buyer. The feature set by tier is not fixed (the brief says so). Right now the pitch reads as "another dispatch tool at a similar price," which is a commodity and ends in a price fight. There is no stated guarantee, no named result, no bonus stack and no reason a buyer would "feel stupid saying no." The fix is a sharper offer (see /founder-offer), but the underlying question is what NeurLead does that the incumbents' own AI features do not. That needs /founder-competitors.
3. **Reachability and cost of the first customers is unknown, and the market is crowded.** The buyers are small owner-operators, busy on job sites, with little time for software evaluation. They are findable, but findable is not the same as cheap to acquire, and self-serve trials of a small-ticket subscription only work if acquisition is cheap and churn is low. Nobody has a CAC, a trial-to-paid rate or a churn figure, and with a $99 entry price the margin for error is thin. A small-ticket SaaS with a high CAC and high churn is a slow loss. This is unknown, not known to be bad, and it must be measured before money goes in (/founder-cfo for the economics, /founder-consumer for the buying reaction).

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. Name three external companies that have used NeurLead through a full trial. What result did each get, in jobs booked or leads answered? How many converted to paid, and if none, why not?
2. What does one extra booked job earn the owner, and how many extra jobs per month does NeurLead produce for Gold Hands? Is that number from the real data? (Open question; the founder's own tenant can answer it, /founder-cfo can turn it into a payback figure.)
3. What exactly is in the $99 tier, the $199 tier and the $399 tier? The brief says this is not fixed. A buyer cannot value an offer that has no edges.
4. What does NeurLead do that Jobber, Housecall Pro, Workiz or a standalone AI dispatcher does not do, today, in a way an owner can see in the first week? (/founder-competitors)
5. Why would an owner switch from the tool they already use, and what do they lose by switching? Is NeurLead a replacement for the field-service software or an add-on to it?
6. What is the guarantee? If NeurLead books no extra jobs in 30 days, what happens? Could you afford to offer that, and at what cost? (/founder-offer, /founder-cfo)
7. How long from sign-up to the first lead handled by the system? How much setup work does the owner do? Effort and time are where the buyer's value drops.
8. Where do the first 100 paying customers come from, and what does each cost? Do you have even one channel that has produced a trial? (/founder-competitors for channels, /founder-consumer for the buyer reaction)
9. Would an owner pay double? What would have to be true for the $399 tier to be an obvious yes, and does any current user ask for it?
10. What is the market size of US home-service companies with 1-10 technicians who would pay for this? Nobody has this number in the brief. It is an open question, and I will not guess it. (/founder-competitors)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Fund only if all of these are met, in this order. None of them needs a number I do not have; the targets below are conditions I am setting, and the founder and the other skills should confirm or adjust them.
1. **Paying proof first.** At least a handful of external, unrelated companies (not friends, not the founder's own company) are paying the full list price after the 14-day trial, and the founder can name them and say what result each got. Until that exists, nothing else should be funded beyond the cost of finding those customers. The exact count is a threshold for the founder to set with /founder-cfo, but zero is a PASS.
2. **A written, tiered feature list and one named result.** Each tier has fixed contents, and the top-line promise is one outcome in the buyer's words (for example, leads answered and jobs booked), not a feature list.
3. **A guarantee the CFO can afford.** A stated guarantee, with the cost of honouring it confirmed by /founder-cfo, so that likelihood is carried by the offer and not only by trust.
4. **A reason to choose NeurLead over the category.** /founder-competitors must identify a gap the incumbents leave, and the offer must be built on it, not on being cheaper.
5. **Measured acquisition economics.** A real trial-to-paid rate, a cost to get one trial and one paying customer, and a churn signal from the first cohort, run through /founder-cfo, showing the payback is acceptable at $99 / $199 / $399. If the numbers fail at the entry price, the board should look at price and tier mix before spending on growth.
6. **Panel check.** /founder-consumer is run on the target owner (1-10 technicians) to see who buys, who does not and why, and the answers feed the offer. Real buyers outrank the panel, but the panel is cheap and should come first.

If condition 1 cannot be met after a fair, time-boxed attempt, the vote becomes PASS on this business as briefed.
