# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- One working tenant: the founder's own company (Gold Hands) runs on it. That is real operating proof that the product functions. It is not proof of demand, because the founder is the one customer who cannot say no.
- A narrow stated target: US home-service owners with 1-10 technicians. A narrow segment is the right shape for a first market. It is not yet shown to be a small market NeurLead owns. It is only a small market NeurLead is aiming at.
- The founder operates a home-service business himself. That could become a "secret" (a non-obvious truth about how small dispatch actually breaks), but it is not stated in the brief. Right now it is a possible source of insight, not a documented one.
- Low-friction entry: self-serve, 14-day trial with no card. This lowers the cost of a first look.
- Replacing the human dispatcher is a sharper claim than "another field-service management tool". If true and measurable, it is a possible 10x on a cost the buyer feels (a salary or the owner's own time). Whether it is true is untested.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Crowded category, no stated defensible edge.** The brief itself lists Workiz, Jobber, Housecall Pro and AI dispatchers as competitors. Competition is what destroys margin, and there is no proprietary technology, network effect, scale advantage or brand named that separates NeurLead. The AI layer is the likeliest thing to be copied: an incumbent with an installed base and a customer list can ship an AI dispatcher feature in a release cycle, and other AI-dispatcher entrants build on the same underlying models. The question "what stops a copycat in month six?" has no answer in the brief. The exact feature set by tier is not even fixed yet (open question), so there is not yet a defined product to defend.
2. **No evidence anyone will pay, and no distribution.** There are zero paying external customers. External trials exist, but a trial with no card is a free look, not demand; no trial-to-paid conversion is on record. The brief names no channel for the first 100 customers, only "online, self-serve via trial". A product with no stated way to reach buyers is not yet a business. Without a repeatable channel, acquisition cost will be set by the incumbents bidding on the same buyers. CAC, trial-to-paid conversion and retention are all unknown.
3. **The market is not shown to be small and owned; it may be neither.** Three price tiers ($99/$199/$399) in a category with entrenched, bundled competitors invites a price comparison, and a price comparison is a commodity fight. "Home-service companies with 1-10 techs" is a broad slice of a crowded category, not a beachhead. Small shops also churn at business-failure rates and can switch cheaply, so durability over ten years is doubtful. The brief does not say why now, why this wedge, or what the first dominated niche is (for example one trade, one metro, one job type).

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. Within the buyer's reach, who else sells a dispatcher for this owner, and why does an owner choose NeurLead over adding an AI feature to the tool already in the truck? (Answered by /founder-competitors.)
2. What is the one small market NeurLead can own first: which trade, which region, which job type? Why that one, and why would it expand from there?
3. What do you know about home-service dispatch that the incumbents and other AI-dispatcher entrants do not? Is it in the product, or only in your head?
4. Which of the four durable advantages (10x proprietary technology, network effects, scale, brand) could this actually build? What is the mechanism, and when does it start?
5. How do the first 100 paying customers arrive, and what does each cost? Is there one channel that works today? (Answered by /founder-consumer for demand and objections, /founder-cfo for CAC against margin.)
6. What happened to the external trials: how many started, how many used it for a week, how many were asked to pay and said no, and what did they say?
7. Is NeurLead 10x better than a human dispatcher or the next AI option on a number the owner cares about (jobs booked, missed calls recovered, hours saved)? Where is that measured, in Gold Hands or elsewhere?
8. Why now? What changed that makes this possible and not already won by incumbents?
9. What are the gross margin and per-tenant AI cost at each tier? Does $99 survive real usage? (Answered by /founder-cfo.)
10. Does the product get better, or stickier, with each customer added (shared data, learned routing, integrations), or is every tenant independent?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable: the vote is PASS on the business as it stands. The vote would move to FUND IF, not FUND, if all of these were evidenced:
- At least a handful of external customers paying real money (not trial users), with a measured trial-to-paid rate and a first-90-day retention figure.
- One narrow beachhead named and dominated first, with a stated reason why that niche is underserved by Workiz, Jobber, Housecall Pro and the AI-dispatcher entrants.
- A documented 10x edge on a metric the owner cares about, shown on customers other than Gold Hands.
- One acquisition channel with a known cost per paying customer that fits inside the margin (/founder-competitors for the gap, /founder-consumer for demand, /founder-cfo for unit economics).
- A named mechanism for durability (data or workflow lock-in, integrations, or a brand in the chosen niche) that is not simply "the model is good".
